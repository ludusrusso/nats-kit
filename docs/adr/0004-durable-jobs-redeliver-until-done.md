# Durable Jobs redeliver until done

A Durable Job is long-running imperative work — reindex a tenant, walk a bucket, rebuild a projection — that outlives the request that asked for it and often the process that starts it. This library makes one such job one JetStream message, published on `durablejobs.jobs.<namespace>.<job>` and held by the broker until a worker acknowledges it as fully done. The two obvious alternatives both reimplement, worse, what the broker already does: a jobs table needs claim, lease, heartbeat and sweeper machinery of its own before two replicas can share it safely, and a Command wrapped in an in-process retry loop keeps its retry state inside the one process doing the work, so a crash or a rolling deploy takes the loop — and the knowledge that the work is unfinished — with it. Under redeliver-until-done, crash recovery is not a feature anyone wrote: it is JetStream's ordinary redelivery of a message that was simply never acked.

The message **is** the continuation, and that has a hard consequence for the job author: `Execute` is re-run from the beginning on every delivery, so it must re-derive its remaining work from durable state — rows still matching a predicate, objects still missing, a status not yet flipped — and never from anything the message carries. A stream message is immutable; progress written into a payload at dispatch time would already be a lie on the second delivery, and there is no way to rewrite it in place. The payload therefore identifies the work (an ID, a scope, a tenant) and nothing else. A job that is not re-runnable will double-apply its work the first time anything redelivers it.

Heartbeats are what separate "still working" from "failed". `AckWait` cannot be sized for work of unknown duration: too short and every long job looks dead, too long and every genuinely dead job stays invisible. So while `Execute` runs, the worker sends `InProgress` at half the `AckWait` cadence. The delivery stays open exactly as long as the job keeps running, a job that is merely slow never burns a delivery, and `MaxDeliver` therefore counts genuine failures only. The heartbeat's `stop` waits for its goroutine to exit before the message is settled, because an `InProgress` landing after the ack would reopen a delivery that is already closed.

Exhaustion is decided from the server's own `NumDelivered`, not from a counter held by the worker, because the decision has to survive the restarts it exists to bound: an in-process counter resets on every crash and every deploy, so a job that poisons its worker would be retried forever — precisely in the situation that produces the restarts. The consumer's server-side `MaxDeliver` is set one above the worker's: the worker dead-letters when `NumDelivered` reaches its own `MaxDeliver`, and the extra delivery is the retry window for the dead-letter hand-off itself. Without it, a crash or a failed DLQ publish on the final attempt would leave the message unacked with no further delivery and no dead letter — work silently lost. A dead-letter publish that fails is therefore naked, so the backstop delivery retries the hand-off; one that succeeds is acked, because the dead letter is now the durable record of the job.

Shutdown is not a verdict on the job. When `Execute` returns an error and the Runner's context is already cancelled, the error describes the process, not the work: the message is naked and the dead-letter decision is skipped entirely, so a rolling deploy landing on a job's final attempt cannot condemn healthy work. Finally, an instance runs one job at a time (`PullMaxMessages(1)`): a Durable Job is heavy by definition, and interleaving two of them on one instance multiplies its memory and I/O for no gain. Parallelism comes from replicas — every replica binds the same durable consumer, `job_<namespace>_<job>`, so the broker hands each message to exactly one of them and failover needs no coordination.

## Considered Options

- **A jobs table with claim, lease and sweeper columns.** Rejected: it rebuilds delivery-to-exactly-one-worker, timeout, redelivery, backlog depth and dead-lettering on top of a database that offers none of them, and every one of those is already a NATS primitive this project depends on anyway.
- **A Command plus an in-process retry loop.** Rejected: a Command is addressed to one handler and settled quickly; stretching one across an hour of work means the retry state, and the fact that the work is unfinished, live only in the process running it. Nothing outside that process can resume it.
- **Chunking: each delivery does a slice of the work and dispatches the next chunk with a cursor in its payload.** Rejected: it puts progress into the payload, which makes every chunk boundary a place to lose or duplicate work, and it dissolves one durable unit into N independent messages whose individual failures no longer add up to the job's failure. Re-deriving the remaining work from the state the job is already writing is both simpler and cheaper.
- **One `AckWait` long enough for the longest imaginable job, instead of heartbeats.** Rejected: `AckWait` is also how long a genuinely crashed job stays invisible, so sizing it for the worst case makes crash recovery arbitrarily slow — and it still breaks for the job that runs longer than imagined.

## Consequences

- `Execute` must be re-runnable, and the payload must never carry a cursor. This is the one obligation this design pushes onto the job author, and nothing in the library can check it.
- A job that hangs forever is heartbeated forever: it never redelivers and never dead-letters. That is the deliberate cost of not guessing a job's duration; the symptom is a delivery that stays ack-pending indefinitely, and the remedy is operational (stop the instance).
- Shutdown waits for every in-flight `Execute` to return, with no timeout at all. A long job delays a deployment by up to its own remaining runtime.
- Failures and dead letters are logged, not counted: this package takes no metrics dependency, so `durablejob: durable job execution failed` and `durablejob: durable job dead-lettered after exhausting deliveries` — both at Error level, both carrying `namespace`, `job` and `delivery` — are the signal to alert on. Any dead letter is work that will not complete without intervention.
- The `durablejobs` stream is converged with `CreateOrUpdateStream` by both `New` and `NewDispatcher`, whichever starts first, which diverges from `natsjs.EnsureStreams`'s rule that an existing stream is never reconfigured. The difference is ownership, not taste: `cqrs-commands`, `cqrs-events` and `cqrs-dlq` are shared infrastructure other services and tooling may rely on, whereas `durablejobs.jobs.>` and `durablejobs.dlq.>` belong wholly to this package. Converging them is safe here, and a later version's subject list applies on the next start rather than waiting on an operator.
- Dead letters live in that same stream, under `durablejobs.dlq.<namespace>.<job>`, carrying the origin subject, the origin stream sequence, the delivery count and the error as headers. They are retained by the stream's own limits; recovering one means republishing its payload on the job subject, and nothing does that automatically.
- Renaming a job, or its namespace, creates a different durable consumer. The old one still exists and still holds whatever unfinished job messages it was given.
